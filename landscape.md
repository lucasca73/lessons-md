I am already amused about landscape composition. As we mix shapes and grayscale depth to compose the layers with so little it already tells so much about the intention.

Some learnings from this introduction are:
- First thing is to define the horizon line, it will guide the other shapes.
- Further shapes has a brighter gray and less details.
- The shape composition guides the viewer's eyes. As where the shapes are pointing or where there is more empty space, it transforms the intention of landscape.
- Round shapes can give a peacefull, relax, completed fealing. Sharp shapes can give more danger, evil, suspicious, intriguing look.
- It is preferable to use hard brushes to make hard edges and clear shapes.
- On Krita after you make the background, make the layer a group and make inner layers to use parent's alpha, it makes it a clip mask for the inner layers.

## Lesson 3 - Lighing Scenarios

Philip shared a really simple technique to add more interest to the landscape. A simple large and soft brush can make a really difference when you apply consistenly across the layers. The exercise consist in exploring 2 variations of light/shadow one closer to the viewer (foreground) and the other highlighting the background. It is funny to see how the mood can change hiding the background or highlighting it.

## Lesson 4 - Atmosphere

On this lesson the main technique is to add fog as the atmosphere impact, so colors from the light source or something that makes the smoke, fog, air color. Use Large soft brushes. Base of the mountains as the atmoshpere has more impact close to the horizon. Can also add the details as sand in the air, or something that is in the air.
